Combat System Overview

Core Fighting Mechanics

Cursed Fate implements a precision-based combat system designed to eliminate RNG frustration while maintaining high skill ceilings. Every interaction is deterministic, readable, and counterable.

Fundamental Mechanics
Mechanic Description Frame Window Dev Notes
Basic Attack (M1) Standard melee combo string. 4-hit chain with diminishing returns on hitstun. 12f startup, 8f recovery 4th hit = soft knockdown. Cannot be canceled into specials after 3rd hit.
Block Universal defensive option. Reduces damage by 70% but builds chip damage. 3f startup, 5f recovery on release Guard break at 100% chip bar. Unblockable attacks bypass entirely.
Dodge/Evasive Spend Evasive Bar for i-frame dodge roll. 8f total duration, 4f i-frames 3 charges, 15s recharge per charge. Directional input affects roll distance.
Parry Frame-perfect deflect. Returns opponent to neutral state. 2f active window Only works on melee attacks. Whiff punishes severely. High risk/reward.
Grab/Throw Command grab that bypasses blocking. 5f startup, 20f recovery Can be tech'd within 10f. Air throws available for specific characters.
Black Flash Critical hit mechanic on perfectly timed strikes. 3-5f timing window 1.5x damage multiplier. Distinct VFX/SFX. Cannot be buffered.
Developer Implementation Notes
  • Hitstop: Apply 8f hitstop on all successful hits for weight feel. 12f on Black Flash.
  • Pushback: Scaling pushback on combo strings prevents infinite combos without resource investment.
  • Damage Fall-off: All moves deal 80% damage after 3rd hit in a combo sequence.
  • Stun Decay: Stun buildup decays at 2% per second when not taking damage.
  • Recovery Cancels: Only special moves can cancel basic attack recovery frames. Ultimates can cancel anything.

Three Bar System

Resource Management Framework

Every character operates on three distinct resource bars that create strategic depth and prevent spam-heavy gameplay. Each bar serves a unique purpose and regenerates differently.

Ultimate Bar
  • Capacity: 100% (fills through damage dealt/taken, time survival)
  • Fill Rate: 0.5% per second passive, 2% per 10 damage dealt, 1% per 15 damage taken
  • Usage: Activates character-specific ultimate ability
  • Duration: Typically 90-150 seconds depending on character
  • Burnout: 15-second cooldown post-ultimate where bar cannot fill
  • VFX: Bar glows white when >80%, pulses at 100%
  • SFX: Rising hum as bar fills, sharp chime at full
Evasive Bar
  • Capacity: 3 charges
  • Recharge: 15 seconds per charge (sequential, not parallel)
  • Usage: Dodge rolls, emergency escapes, certain movement options
  • Depletion: Instant on use
  • VFX: Individual pips fade as charges used, refill animation per charge
  • SFX: Soft click on use, ascending tones on recharge complete
  • Strategic Note: Running dry leaves player vulnerable to pressure
Technique Bar (R-Skill)
  • Capacity: Character-dependent (some have CD, some have charges)
  • Cooldown Types: Fixed CD (4-8s), Charge-based (2-3 stacks), Conditional (reset on success)
  • Usage: Signature techniques, stance changes, unique mechanics
  • VFX: Bar depletes on use, refills based on cooldown type
  • SFX: Unique per character, typically elemental or technique-specific
  • Balance: Primary tool for neutral game control
Resource Balance Philosophy
  • Never allow all three bars to synergize into an "unstoppable" state simultaneously.
  • Ultimate should feel impactful but not automatically win-neutral.
  • Evasive bar must be scarce enough to matter but frequent enough to not feel oppressive.
  • Technique bar defines character identity—make it spammable only if low-impact.
  • Visual clarity is paramount: players must always know their resource state at a glance.

Frame Data Notation Guide

Startup: Frames before first active frame. Lower = faster.
Active: Frames where hitbox can connect. Multiple active windows possible.
Recovery: Frames after last active frame before return to neutral.
Total: Startup + Active + Recovery.
On Block: Frame advantage/disadvantage when blocked. Negative = punishable.
On Hit: Frame advantage on successful hit. Positive = can continue pressure.

Critical Frame Data Rules
  • No move shall exceed 30f startup unless balanced by extreme range, damage, or utility.
  • Punishable moves (<-10 on block) must provide clear telegraphing.
  • Safe moves (-5 or better) must have limited range or low damage.
  • All combos must have a hard ender by hit 6 unless ultimate is active.
  • True blockstrings cannot exceed 12 hits without spending resources.
  • Infinite combo prevention: all hitstun scales down 10% per consecutive hit.

Hitbox Guidelines

Collision Detection Standards

Consistent, readable hitboxes are essential for competitive integrity. All hitboxes must follow these specifications to ensure fair play and predictable interactions.

Hitbox Categories
Type Dimensions Use Case Visual Feedback
Melee Point 3-5 stud radius sphere Fists, weapons, close-range strikes White flash on contact, 8f hitstop
Melee Arc 8-12 stud arc, 45-90° angle Swinging weapons, wide slashes Trail effect matching weapon color
Projectile Small 2-3 stud sphere Energy bolts, thrown objects Glowing core with particle trail
Projectile Large 5-8 stud sphere Heavy projectiles, AoE blasts Expanding ring on impact
Beam Linear 2-4 stud width, up to 50 stud length Laser attacks, energy beams Continuous beam with pulse effect
AoE Circle 10-25 stud radius Ground slams, explosion zones Expanding ripple from center
AoE Cone 15-30 stud length, 60-120° angle Forward cones, breath attacks Fan-shaped particle burst
Grab Zone 5-8 stud frontal hemisphere Command grabs, throws Hand/gesture indicator, red highlight on target
Sure-Hit Variable (bypasses traditional collision) Domain expansions, guaranteed hits Screen-wide effect, distinct color shift
Hitbox Implementation Rules
  • All hitboxes must have corresponding hurtboxes on character models (head, torso, limbs).
  • Hitbox duration must match stated active frames exactly—no hidden extensions.
  • Multi-hit moves must have minimum 3f gaps between hit activations.
  • Projectile hitboxes must persist for entire travel distance or have clear dissipation point.
  • AoE hitboxes must have visible ground indicators for 0.5s before activation.
  • Grab hitboxes cannot intersect with wall geometry—auto-fail if obstructed.
  • Hitbox size cannot scale with damage—what you see is what you get.
  • All hitboxes must be testable in dev mode with debug visualization toggle.
Debug Tools for Hitbox Testing
  • F1: Toggle hitbox visualization (green = active, red = hurtbox, yellow = projectile)
  • F2: Show frame counter overlay
  • F3: Display damage numbers with hitstun values
  • F4: Slow-motion mode (0.25x, 0.5x, 0.75x toggles)
  • F5: Reset character positions to neutral spacing
  • Console Command: /hitboxdebug [character] [move] for isolated testing

VFX Performance Budget
  • Maximum 50 active particle systems per player at any time.
  • Ultimate sequences capped at 200 simultaneous particles.
  • All textures must be power-of-2 dimensions (64x64, 128x128, 256x256 max).
  • Shader complexity: maximum 200 instructions per pixel shader.
  • LOD system: reduce particle count by 50% at 50+ stud distance.
  • Mobile optimization: automatic 40% reduction in particle density.
  • No alpha-blended overlapping particles beyond 3 layers.
  • All VFX must pass 60fps test in 20-player stress scenario.

Audio Mixing Guidelines
  • Master volume: combat SFX at -6dB, UI SFX at -12dB, music at -18dB.
  • Dynamic range compression: 4:1 ratio on impact sounds to prevent clipping.
  • Priority system: KO/ultimate SFX always override ambient/footstep sounds.
  • 3D audio: all positional sounds must use spatial audio with 50-stud falloff.
  • Voice lines: duck music by -10dB during character dialogue.
  • Loop management: seamless loops for sustained effects (beams, auras).
  • Platform optimization: mono fallback for mobile, stereo minimum, 5.1 surround optional.
  • File format: .wav for development, compressed .ogg for build (quality 0.7).
